What is an Infrastructure Control Plane?

An infrastructure control plane is a centralized system that maintains the intended configuration of infrastructure resources and continuously compares it against the actual state observed in the environments it manages. InfraSphere is designed as an independent, self-hosted control plane so organizations retain ownership of their operational model across multi-cloud and hybrid environments.

Why multi-cloud management matters: organizations run workloads across multiple public clouds, private clouds, virtualization platforms, and on-premises systems. Without a unified control plane, each environment is managed with separate tools, leading to inconsistent policy, configuration drift, and limited visibility. Provider independence reduces dependence on any single vendor’s management console.

Capabilities

Multi-Environment Management

Centralized operational model across public cloud, virtualization, containers, and on-premises.

Purpose: One place to define and observe infrastructure across heterogeneous environments.
Inputs: Environment connections and resource definitions.
Outputs: Unified inventory and change coordination.
Relationship: Feeds desired-state and reconciliation.

Desired-State Management

Express intended configuration once and maintain it as the source of operational intent.

Purpose: Desired state is the authoritative declaration of what infrastructure should look like.
Inputs: Desired specifications.
Outputs: Comparison baseline for observed state.
Relationship: Compared against observed state by reconciliation.

Continuous Reconciliation

Identify differences between desired and observed state and support controlled alignment.

Purpose: Detect and report drift so operators can restore consistency under policy control.
Inputs: Desired and observed snapshots.
Outputs: Drift reports and change proposals.
Relationship: Uses policy evaluation before any change is authorized.

Policy and Governance

Evaluate changes against organizational policy before they are applied.

Purpose: Ensure infrastructure changes stay within approved boundaries.
Inputs: Proposed changes.
Outputs: Allow, deny, or require-approval decisions.
Relationship: Gates reconciliation actions.

Infrastructure Visibility and Intelligence

Understand resource relationships, change impact, and operational health.

Purpose: Provide the context needed for safe change and troubleshooting.
Inputs: Inventory and observations.
Outputs: Dependency and health views.
Relationship: Supports architecture and security analysis.

Hybrid Infrastructure Operations

Apply one operational model across cloud and on-premises infrastructure.

Purpose: Avoid separate processes for cloud and private environments.
Inputs: Mixed environment targets.
Outputs: Consistent control and reporting.
Relationship: Extends multi-environment management beyond public cloud.

      How the Operational Model Works

      1. Infrastructure providers appear

      Environments are registered as targets for management.

      2. Connections form

      Connectors collect observed state from each environment.

      3. Control plane activates

      Desired and observed state are held side by side.

      4. Policy evaluation

      Proposed changes are checked against governance rules.

      5. Drift becomes visible

      Differences are identified and reported.

      6. Reconciliation progresses

      Authorized changes move the environment toward desired state.

      7. Consistency is verified

      Observed state is re-checked against desired state.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is InfraSphere?

      InfraSphere is an independent, self-hosted multi-cloud and hybrid infrastructure control plane designed for desired-state management, governance, and operational consistency across heterogeneous environments.

      What is an independent infrastructure control plane?

      An infrastructure control plane maintains the intended configuration of resources and compares it against the observed state in the environments it manages. Independence means the control plane is not tied to any single cloud provider’s management tools.

      How does multi-cloud infrastructure management work?

      Multi-cloud management provides a unified operational model across different public cloud environments so policy, inventory, and change processes are consistent rather than fragmented by provider console.

      What is desired-state infrastructure management?

      Desired-state management expresses the intended configuration of infrastructure once and uses that declaration as the baseline for comparison against the actual observed state.

      What is infrastructure reconciliation?

      Reconciliation is the process of identifying differences between desired and observed state, evaluating them against policy, and supporting controlled alignment. Full closed-loop reconciliation across all environments remains a design objective.

      What is configuration drift?

      Configuration drift occurs when the actual observed configuration of infrastructure differs from the intended desired state, for example missing resources, unexpected changes, or mismatched settings.

      Does InfraSphere support hybrid infrastructure?

      Yes. Hybrid infrastructure management is a core design goal so one operational model can apply across public cloud, virtualization, container platforms, and on-premises environments.

      Is InfraSphere self-hosted?

      Yes. InfraSphere is designed as a self-hosted control plane so organizations retain ownership of their operational model.

      Which infrastructure providers are supported?

      AWS, Azure, GCP, Kubernetes, and Proxmox are in development. VMware and on-premises / private cloud are planned. Statuses reflect current maturity; nothing is marked production-ready unless verified.

      Is InfraSphere production-ready?

      InfraSphere is under active development. Certain provider adapters and capabilities exist in various stages of maturity. It should not be treated as fully production-ready across all environments until explicitly verified.
